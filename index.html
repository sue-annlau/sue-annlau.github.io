How I got here

Six cities, three continents.

My path hasn't been a straight line. I studied history at NYU and was set on a PhD, because I loved research and writing. Then, while interning at a state museum in object acquisitions and curation, I watched the museum get outbid by private individuals. We weren't successful in adding that object to the collection. I wanted to know how that was financially possible, and why. That question sent me down the rabbit hole of art financing, which lives in the world of private banking.

That's what brought me to a global bank in Singapore, managing relationships for ultra-high-net-worth clients. Side projects for the CEO's office turned into a role there, leading transformation work through a leadership transition across Asia.

After five years, I wanted to see how the same skills worked somewhere smaller and faster. That took me to Kellogg for my MBA, product management at a global golf equipment manufacturer and a VC-backed sports tech startup.

Today I'm Chief of Staff at a PE-backed mobile game company, working at the intersection of strategy, growth and operations.

Selected work

A few problems I've worked on, and how I approached them

PE-backed mobile game | Building with AI

An early-warning system for our player community

The problem: players raise bugs and outages in our Discord first, and I noticed we only caught them if someone happened to be reading at the right moment

What I did: built a fix with AI, starting with something just for me and improving it each round:

  • v1: a 5-minute daily digest of what players talked about the day before, sent to my email
  • v1.5: runs every hour on my laptop and emails me if it spots outages, security incidents or common themes in player feedback

Up next: a bot that runs 24/7 and alerts the whole team

PE-backed mobile game | Operating rhythm

From monthly numbers to the boardroom

The problem: finance, marketing and product each worked from their own numbers and sources, and turning it into clear decisions for leadership and the board meant starting from scratch every time.

What I did: built a reporting cadence where each layer feeds the next

  • Monthly: a five-page pack that puts financials beside revenue drivers and player health, with commentary on what moved and why
  • Quarterly: board meetings and business reviews with stakeholders, built on the monthly numbers, with a narrative framed around the decisions in front of us

Result: one source of truth and one story; the numbers show what happened; the commentary explains why and what we're doing about it

VC-backed sports tech startup | Go-to-market

Identifying another channel of growth

The problem: diversify revenue by selling through businesses, with no partnership program to start from

What I did:

  • used segmentation analysis to spot club-fitting studios as a strong fit; they needed flexibility, not a permanent install, which is exactly what a mobile simulator offered
  • designed the program end-to-end: pricing that works for both sides, setup logistics, and recommended a national golf retailer as the first partner for visibility and credibility

Result: a playbook built to scale across the startup's franchise network; it went on to launch as a pilot with that retailer

Global golf equipment manufacturer | Product strategy

Winning value-focused golfers without lowering the price

The problem: the brand already had a value golf ball, but it wasn't winning the share it should

What I did: surveyed golfers to understand how they shop for value balls, and studied how competing brands at the same price point were packaging and merchandising theirs

Result: recommended a new SKU pack format that gave golfers more balls for their money without cutting the price

Global private bank | Change management

From paper to app, one banker and client at a time

The problem: bankers and clients still did everything on paper; fields got missed, forms bounced back, compliance checks stalled waiting on incomplete paperwork

What I did: digitized the forms with Operations and Regulators, then worked directly with bankers to change how they worked with clients; showing them how the app and paperless signing made their days easier, not harder

Result: double-digit growth in app usage, digital trade confirmations and paperless signing across the region's bankers

Global private bank | 0 to 1

Building a client segment from zero

The problem: high-net-worth clients were a major opportunity, but the bank only had a dedicated offering for the tier above them

What I did:

  • sized the market, defined who these clients were and pinpointed how their needs differed from ultra-wealthy and everyday banking clients
  • with BizOps, Sales and Product, designed what launch would look like: how clients would be acquired, how the team would be resourced and what would make the offering stand out

Result: a launch roadmap with sales coverage and a client pipeline in place, built to support fast growth
